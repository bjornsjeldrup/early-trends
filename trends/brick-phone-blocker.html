Brick phone blocker

A small phone blocker, about $59, riding a wave of people who want their phone to stay quiet. Mid as a cultural product. Not a medical device, and not a treatment for anything.

Where to buy

Identified on October 28, 2026 (2026-10-28)

Signals are not guarantees. A sell-out, a search spike or a celebrity sighting can fade. This page describes public reporting. It is not a forecast, and it is not a reason to buy anything as an investment.

Mid

What it is

Brick is a phone blocker sold around $59. People use it to make a phone harder to pick up — a physical or app-linked friction, depending on the setup — alongside dumb phones and other blockers. We are covering it as a habit tool in a shopping category. That is the whole claim.

Signals we can actually cite

  • A reported wave, not a clinical result: Hindustan Times, Fortune, and a 2026 tech-trends roundup all describe people trying to use their phones less, with Brick named among app blockers and analog habits. Fortune’s piece groups it with dumb phones and social-media limits.
  • What we will not say: we make no health claim. Brick is a habit tool. If a source frames heavy phone use in medical language, that wording is theirs, not a result we are reporting.

Not a health product. If you need help with phone use that is hurting you, talk to a person who is qualified to give it. This page is a trend note about a gadget.

Why this stage and meter

The sources agree that a phone-free wave is underway and that blockers, including Brick, are part of how people are acting on it. That is media and cultural coverage, which we read as a seed-plus-momentum signal, not as proof of effect. No download total, no clinical result, and no sell-out figure appears in the way we are willing to quote. Meter: 2/5. Stage: mid, because the behavior is already a reported wave rather than a single prototype.
