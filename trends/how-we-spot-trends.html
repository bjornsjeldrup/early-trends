How we spot trends early

A plain-English guide to the four core public signals we watch, the supporting sources we add per category, how they stack up, and where they mislead. Educational only — this describes a process, not a track record.

Important: nothing on this page claims past results or predicts future ones. Signals are clues, not guarantees. Many products show all four and still fade.

The idea in one paragraph

Viral consumer trends rarely appear from nowhere. Usually a small “seed” (a well-known person or tight-knit community) makes something visible, short-form video multiplies that visibility, curious people start searching for it, and finally demand outruns supply — which shows up as sell-outs and resale prices. Watching for those stages, in roughly that order, is the whole method.

  1. Celebrity & community seeds

    A musician, athlete, streamer or influential hobbyist is seen with a product — often casually, without it being an ad. Fan communities notice, ask “what is that?” and start sharing.

    What we look for: organic (non-sponsored) appearances, repeat sightings, and fans actively identifying the item. Caveat: one sighting is noise; paid placements are not a signal.

  2. TikTok & short-video acceleration

    Unboxings, “haul” videos, reactions and DIY spin-offs start to repeat across unrelated accounts. The format spreads, not just one video.

    What we look for: many small and mid-sized creators posting independently, growth in a hashtag’s video count over days or weeks, and the product appearing in other niches. Caveat: a single viral clip without follow-ups often fades.

  3. Google Trends breakouts

    Curiosity turns into search. In Google Trends, a related query labelled “Breakout” means search interest grew very sharply from a low base.

    What we look for: breakout or steeply rising related queries, interest spreading across several countries, and shopping-style searches (“where to buy”, “restock”). Caveat: Trends shows relative interest, not absolute volume — a breakout can still be small.

  4. Reseller prices & sell-outs

    When official stock sells out quickly, listings on resale marketplaces appear above retail price. That gap is a rough measure of unmet demand.

    What we look for: completed (sold) listings rather than asking prices, restocks selling out, and queues or purchase limits at retailers. Caveat: resale spikes can be driven by scarcity tactics and can collapse fast — we never recommend buying to resell.

How the signals combine

We give each trend a signal rating out of five, roughly one point per signal that is clearly present plus one for momentum across signals. It’s a way to summarize what we see, not a score with predictive power.

Case study: Labubu

Labubu is a mischievous, toothy elf-like character created by Hong Kong-born artist Kasing Lung as part of his The Monsters universe, and sold as collectible figures and plush charms by Pop Mart, often in “blind boxes” where you don’t know which design you’ll get.

Looking back through the lens of the four signals:

  • Seed: the character had a loyal designer-toy following for years. In 2024, widely shared photos of BLACKPINK’s Lisa with Labubu charms introduced it to a huge new audience.
  • Acceleration: unboxing and “bag charm” videos multiplied on short-video platforms, with fans showing off collections and outfits for their figures.
  • Search: public search interest for the name rose sharply as people asked what it was and where to buy one.
  • Resale: widely reported sell-outs, queues and resale prices above retail showed demand outrunning supply.

This is written in hindsight — it is easy to connect the dots afterwards. The point is to learn what the pattern looked like, not to suggest anyone “called it.” We did not cover Labubu before its rise and make no claims about having done so.

What we won’t cover

We skip anything adult, drug- or vape-related, unsafe for kids, or built on hype schemes. If a trend is mostly about flipping for profit, we’ll mention that — and say why we’re cautious about it.
