Early Trends: the side project trying to predict what’s under the tree this year, and admitting when it’s wrong

A small independent project spots consumer products before they go mainstream, using public early signals. This note is the 2026 holiday list: five early picks, and an honest count of how late the first batch was.

The short story

Early Trends watches public early signals and writes up consumer products while they are still easy to miss. A signal here is something a reader can open: a press release, a deals post, a toy-trade note, a search phrase that was quiet and then was not. The method page stays general on purpose.

The project is independent and small. It is not a lab and it is not a store. Where a product page has a shop link, that link is Amazon only. This press note links the product pages.

The first batch of picks was mostly too late. Every miss changed the rules the next page had to follow. The scorecard below is published so a reader can see whether later batches are earlier. It is a count of what already happened. It is not a promise about the next one.

Honest scorecard

We have published 29 picks. 16 turned out to be too late, already mainstream when we called them. 13 are still early. None has fizzled yet. Our first batch (22 picks, Oct 28) was mostly too late: 13 of 22.

The misses stay public. Our misses is where a late pick goes once a genuinely earlier one has taken its place. The NeeDoh case study is the clearest example of calling something early when it was already everywhere. Each miss changed the rules. The scorecard stays up so a reader can see whether we get better. We are not promising any future accuracy percentage.

We’ll check back in January

In January we will publish how these five did: still early, already mainstream, or faded. That note will use the same kind of public signals. It will not be a promised hit rate.
